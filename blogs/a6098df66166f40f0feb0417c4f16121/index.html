Ephyra: A Property-Based Testing Framework for the LLM Era
arXiv Query: search_query=all:"software testing"&id_list=&start=0&max_results=10
Coding agents now write a growing share of software tests; however, property-based testing (PBT) libraries are designed with historical considerations. We present Ephyra, a PBT library in Rust built on three concepts: customizability, observability and generator synthesis. Ephyra lets users compose their own testing loops from generation, mutation and shrinking, and synthesize these components to satisfy functional predicates. Applying Ephyra to 14 open source systems, we have found 349 bugs and reported 300 of them, in database management systems (SQLite, Turso, PostgreSQL, DuckDB, DataFusion), the verified policy engine (Cedar), the SQL equivalence prover (QED), the URL parser used in Node.js (ada-url), reimplementations of existing open source systems in Rust (coreutils, brush, gitoxide), and compilers (binaryen, OCaml, and OxCaml).

Noise-Awareness in Quantum Software Testing: An Empirical Landscape Analysis
arXiv Query: search_query=all:"software testing"&id_list=&start=0&max_results=10
Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware has transformed quantum computing into a practical experimental platform while introducing fundamental challenges associated with hardware noise, decoherence, and probabilistic execution. Although quantum software testing (QST) has rapidly emerged as an active research area, it remains unclear to what extent existing testing methodologies explicitly engage with noisy and hardware-constrained execution environments. This paper presents a systematic empirical landscape analysis of noise-awareness in QST research through a deterministic, taxonomy-guided analytical framework applied to 61 papers published between 2018 and 2025 across eight testing paradigms, contrasting how contributions are framed with how they are evaluated. Among the analyzed papers, 47 (77.0%) are noise-oblivious and only 14 (23.0%) explicitly engage with noise-related execution concerns, while only 11 (18.0%) report evaluation on physical hardware or a noisy simulator.
